**UAE content law that applies to AcesBook itself** (not only to gaming operators):

- **Advertiser Permit.** The UAE Media Council's permit has been mandatory since 1 February 2026 for anyone publishing promotional content on social media, paid or unpaid. Fines run up to AED 1 million [S16, S17].
- **Media law content standards.** The 2025 media law sets 20 mandatory content standards, with fines from AED 10,000 to AED 1 million [S18].
- **Cybercrime law.** Federal Decree-Law 34/2021 penalises online promotion of gambling outside the law [S21, S22]. The exact article and penalty are **[LEGAL-CHECK]**; the sources disagree on the detail.
- **Open legal question.** It is not established whether an informational channel run from the UAE needs a Media Council *media licence*, as opposed to an advertiser permit. **[LEGAL-CHECK, blocking before any public launch].**

## 2.2 Platform rules that bite

- **Telegram.** Gambling is reported as prohibited in Telegram Ads [S30, secondary]. So *paid* Telegram growth for a gambling-themed brand is likely unavailable. Telegram has also been pulled from app stores over content issues, including gambling-related ones [S34].
- **WhatsApp.** Meta's business messaging policy allows messages that *promote* real-money gambling only with prior written permission, and only where legal [S29]. A news channel that does not promote may be fine, but enforcement is at Meta's discretion.
- **Meta, Google, X ads.** Gambling-adjacent ads in the UAE were re-tightened in 2026 [S19, secondary]. Paid acquisition must promote the *news brand*, never betting.

# 3. Positioning (JARVIS proposal)

**Promise:** the GCC's verified briefing on gaming: regulation, resorts, lottery, esports and the business behind them, in English and Arabic.

**Editorial red lines (proposed, for G to confirm):**
1. No odds, tips, predictions, bonus codes, affiliate links, or "how to bet / how to sign up" content.
2. No links to any operator's betting or registration pages. Links go to regulators, official press releases and news outlets.
3. Every licensee mention states who licenses it and the access limits, for example "18+/21+, UAE residents, not available in Dubai" where the source says so.
4. Responsible-gambling note on any story about a gambling product.
5. Winners' stories use only names the operator has published. No speculation about private people.
6. Content on prohibited states is limited to law, enforcement and economic news. Nothing normalises participation.
7. Every factual sentence carries a source. Single-source claims are labelled. Unverifiable claims are not published.

# 6. Editorial operations and AI pipeline

1. **Scan.** Twice daily, the AI scans a whitelist of sources: regulators (GCGRA), official operator press rooms, WAM, the major UAE dailies and trade press.
2. **Select.** The AI proposes 8–12 candidates per stream with a reason and an audience fit. Each desk's rules (§5) filter them.
3. **Draft.** The AI writes the post in the stream's own language. AR is drafted in Arabic, not translated.
4. **Verify.** Each claim is matched to a source. Claims of numbers, names and dates need a primary source or two independent ones. Conflicts are flagged. Examples found during this review:
   - the GCGRA founding year is 2023 in most sources but 2022 in some [S6, S1];
   - the Esports World Cup 2026 is placed in Riyadh by the organiser [S23] but in Paris by a tracker [S24]. JARVIS uses the organiser.
5. **Compliance check.** The red lines (§3), the legal checklist, rights to images, and personal data.
6. **Human approval and publish.** Outside the AI until G authorises.
7. **Log.** Source URLs, verification status, approver and publish time.

**Rights:** use only own graphics, licensed stock, or official press images where the press terms allow it. Do not take images from other channels, Dubai Decoded included.
